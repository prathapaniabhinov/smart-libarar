Forest Ecology

Chapter 1: The Forest as a System
A forest is a community of trees, shrubs, animals, fungi and microorganisms living together.
Energy enters the forest through sunlight, which leaves capture during photosynthesis.
Nutrients cycle from the soil into plants, then back to the soil when leaves and wood decay.

Chapter 2: Layers of the Forest
The canopy is the upper layer formed by the crowns of the tallest trees.
Below the canopy is the understorey, where young trees and shrubs grow in partial shade.
The forest floor is covered with fallen leaves, which decomposers break down into humus.

Chapter 3: How a Tree Grows
A tree grows taller from buds at the tips of its branches and wider from a thin layer called the cambium.
Each year the cambium adds a new ring of wood, so counting rings tells the age of a tree.
The roots of a tree anchor it in the soil and absorb water and minerals.
Leaves release water vapour through small pores, which pulls more water up from the roots.

Chapter 4: Forest Types
Tropical rainforests are warm and wet all year and contain the greatest variety of species.
Deciduous forests lose their leaves in autumn and grow new leaves in spring.
Coniferous forests are made of cone bearing trees such as pine, spruce and fir.
India has tropical evergreen, tropical deciduous, mangrove and mountain forests.

Chapter 5: Why Forests Matter
Forests store carbon and help regulate the climate.
They protect the soil from erosion and keep rivers and springs flowing.
Forests provide timber, fruit, medicine and a home for wildlife.

Chapter 6: Protecting Forests
Deforestation, forest fires and overgrazing reduce forest cover.
Planting native trees, protecting watersheds and involving local communities help forests recover.
